# Pact — Business & Product Vision

## 1. Product Thesis

**Pact turns intention into commitment.**

Vyana helps users understand their signals, recognize patterns, and choose practices that may improve their wellbeing.

Pact adds the accountability layer:

> **Vyana helps you know what to change. Pact helps you actually do it.**

A Pact is a commitment between a person and their future self, their friends, their community, or a sponsor.

The participant defines:

- What they are committing to
- How long the commitment lasts
- What counts as success
- How completion can be verified
- Who can participate
- What, if anything, is at stake
- What happens when participants succeed or fail

The financial stake is only one possible incentive mechanism.

The broader product is built around:

**Commitment → Proof → Outcome → Consequence**

---

# 2. Product Positioning

Pact should not be presented primarily as:

> **Bet money on completing habits.**

That creates unnecessary gambling associations and makes the product feel narrower than it is.

The broader positioning is:

> # Bet on the person you want to become.

And socially:

> # Back someone you believe in.

Pact makes commitments meaningful by putting something behind them.

Sometimes that's money.

Sometimes it's coffee.

Sometimes it's a reward from someone who believes in you.

Sometimes it's simply your word.

The common denominator is not the stake.

**It's commitment.**

---

# 3. Pact Types

| Pact Type             | Example                                                        | Primary Motivation     |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Personal Stake**    | “I put $25 behind completing this.”                            | Self-accountability    |
| **Friends Pool**      | 5 friends put $10 each; finishers split the failed stakes      | Group accountability   |
| **Friendly Forfeit**  | Loser buys coffee, pizza or dinner                             | Fun/social pressure    |
| **Reward Pact**       | “Complete 20 workouts and I'll buy you dinner.”                | Positive reinforcement |
| **Sponsor Pact**      | A friend puts $50 behind your goal; you earn it if you finish  | Support                |
| **Team Pact**         | Everyone contributes; successful participants share the reward | Community              |
| **Organization Pact** | Employer funds rewards for completing a wellbeing program      | Employee engagement    |
| **No-Money Pact**     | Streak, badge, recognition or social accountability only       | Habit formation        |

These should all sit on top of a common commitment model rather than becoming separate products.

# 4. Core Consumer Modes

The Pact types can be simplified into four clear entry points for users.

## 4.1 Just Me

**Make a Pact with yourself.**

Example:

> Meditate for at least 15 minutes on 11 of the next 14 days.

The user may:

- Stake money
- Create a personal consequence
- Create a personal reward
- Complete the Pact without financial stakes

This is the lowest-friction introduction to Pact.

---

## 4.2 With Friends

**Make the same commitment together.**

Example:

> Five friends commit to walking 8,000 steps on at least 20 of the next 30 days.

Possible settlement:

- Everyone who succeeds receives their stake back
- Stakes belonging to unsuccessful participants form a reward pool
- Successful participants divide that pool
- If everyone succeeds, everyone gets their original stake back

The ideal outcome should be that **everyone succeeds**.

The psychology should not be:

> “I hope my friend fails so I get their money.”

It should be:

> **“We both want to win. But if you quit, I'm taking your coffee money.”**

---

## 4.3 Challenge Someone

**Same goal. Friendly stakes.**

Example:

> Who can complete at least 12 meditation sessions over the next 14 days?

Possible stakes:

- $10 each
- Coffee
- Pizza
- Dinner
- A custom reward
- Bragging rights

This creates a lightweight social challenge without requiring users to understand crypto or financial contracts.

---

## 4.4 Back Someone

**Put something behind someone you believe in.**

Example:

> Anna commits to being in bed before 11 PM on at least 24 of the next 30 nights.

Friends can back Anna:

- Friend A — coffee
- Friend B — $20
- Partner — dinner
- Employer — $50 wellness credit

If Anna completes her Pact, she unlocks the pledged rewards.

The core framing is:

> **Don't bet on whether someone fails. Back them to succeed.**

This can become one of Pact's strongest social and viral mechanics.

---

# 5. The Pact Economy

Pact should distinguish between **financial value** and **social value**.

## 5.1 Financial Stakes

Participants can put an approved asset behind a commitment.

Examples:

- Stablecoins
- Approved ecosystem tokens
- Future platform credits

The settlement rules are defined before the Pact starts.

Potential business models include:

- Small settlement fee
- Percentage of redistributed failed stakes
- Premium financial Pact creation
- Sponsored Pact administration fees

The fee should remain secondary to the user experience.

Pact should make money because users are committing, completing programs, and creating activity — not because the platform wants them to fail.

---

## 5.2 Social Stakes

Many Pacts should require no financial asset.

Examples:

### Loser buys

- Coffee
- Pizza
- Dinner
- Movie tickets

### Winner gets

- Custom reward
- Experience
- Gift
- Recognition

Pact records the agreement and verified outcome.

Fulfillment can initially happen outside the platform.

This allows users to experience Pact before ever interacting with blockchain infrastructure.

---

## 5.3 Sponsored Rewards

A third party funds the incentive.

Possible sponsors include:

- Friends
- Family
- Wellness coaches
- Retreat organizers
- Gyms
- Brands
- Employers
- Wellness partners

Example:

> Complete Seek Nirvana's 21-Day Sleep Reset and receive a $25 wellness credit.

This creates a broader business opportunity beyond peer-to-peer challenges.

---

# 6. Vyana × Pact

Pact becomes substantially more valuable when integrated directly into Vyana rather than existing as a separate challenge application.

Seek Nirvana already positions Vyana as the layer that interprets a user's private signals and turns them into practical daily guidance. Pact extends that journey from understanding and practice into commitment.

Example:

**Vyana**

> Your bedtime has moved about 45 minutes later over the past two weeks.

> Moving it earlier gradually may help your routine.

### Suggested Pact

**7-Day Earlier Night**

> Be in bed before 11:45 PM on at least 5 of the next 7 nights.

Actions:

- **Make a Pact**
- **Invite a friend**
- **Ask someone to back me**

This creates the loop:

**Signals → Understanding → Practice → Commitment → Proof → Reflection**

That fits naturally with Seek Nirvana's existing emphasis on building better daily rituals rather than merely showing users more metrics.

---

# 7. Privacy & Proof

Pact should prove **completion**, not expose the health data underneath it.

For example, another participant should see:

> **Today's commitment completed ✓**

They should not see:

> Sleep: 5h 42m\
> HRV: 39 ms\
> Resting HR: 78 bpm\
> Bedtime: 11:21 PM

The user's device and Vyana determine whether the agreed Pact condition has been satisfied.

The Pact receives only the proof necessary to establish the outcome.

Conceptually:

> `requirementSatisfied = true`

rather than:

> `sleepDuration = 20,520 seconds`

This keeps Pact aligned with Seek Nirvana's existing privacy positioning, where personal signals belong to the individual and organizations do not get access to individual health data.

# 8. Consumer Business Model

Pact does not necessarily need to monetize every challenge.

Its first business role may be to increase:

- Engagement
- Retention
- Social acquisition
- Program completion
- Subscription value

## 8.1 Free

Possible free functionality:

- Personal Pacts
- Basic friend challenges
- Non-financial rewards
- Small private groups
- Basic proof tracking

## 8.2 Premium

Vyana premium could add:

- Advanced Pact creation
- Larger groups
- Vyana-generated Pact recommendations
- Longer challenges
- Advanced completion insights
- Private communities
- Expert-led Pacts
- More complex reward structures

Pact therefore becomes another reason to remain inside Vyana rather than just another standalone revenue stream.

---

# 9. Transaction Revenue

Financial Pacts can generate a small protocol or settlement fee.

One possible principle:

### Successful participant

Receives their principal back.

### Failed participant

Their committed stake is handled according to the predefined Pact rules.

For pooled Pacts, a small portion of redistributed value can become protocol revenue.

Exact percentages should be determined later through user testing, economic modeling, regulatory analysis, and market positioning.

The business should avoid creating an incentive where Pact benefits disproportionately from user failure.

---

# 10. Organization Pacts

Organizations are a particularly important business opportunity because Pact can become the engagement layer for sponsored wellbeing programs.

Seek Nirvana already positions its organizational offering around participation and programs without giving employers access to individual health signals.

Example:

> ## 30-Day Better Sleep Pact

Company sponsors 100 employees.

Employees voluntarily opt in.

Each employee commits privately to completing the program.

Vyana verifies completion.

The employer may see:

- Number enrolled
- Participation rate
- Completion rate
- Aggregate reward spend

The employer should not see:

- Individual sleep data
- HRV
- Heart rate
- Stress/readiness
- Journal entries
- Individual behavioral patterns

---

## 10.1 Organization Reward Pool

Example:

**$5,000 wellness reward pool**

100 employees participate.

67 successfully complete the program.

Rewards could include:

- Wellness credits
- Seek Nirvana subscriptions
- Expert sessions
- Retreat credits
- Partner rewards
- Monetary rewards where appropriate

Seek Nirvana could charge for:

**Seats + Program + Reward Administration**

This may become a stronger B2B revenue model than relying on consumer transaction fees alone.

---

# 11. Expert & Creator Pacts

Seek Nirvana experts, psychologists, wellness coaches and program creators can create structured Pacts.

Example:

> ## 21-Day Sleep Reset

The expert defines:

- Daily practices
- Commitment requirements
- Proof rules
- Educational content
- Group sessions
- Completion criteria

Participants join the program and optionally attach a Pact.

This transforms the model from:

**Content → Hope they complete it**

into:

**Program → Commitment → Verified Participation → Completion**

Possible economics:

**Participant payment → Platform share + Expert share**

Optional stakes and sponsored rewards can sit on top.

This aligns with Seek Nirvana's existing use of experts and cohort programs as a core part of the product rather than treating human support as an afterthought.

# 13. Growth Loops

Pact has several natural acquisition loops.

## 13.1 Challenge Loop

User creates Pact\
→ invites friend\
→ friend joins Vyana\
→ both participate\
→ one or both create another Pact

## 13.2 Backing Loop

User creates Pact\
→ shares it\
→ friends back them\
→ friends follow progress\
→ completion is celebrated\
→ backers create their own Pact

## 13.3 Program Loop

Expert creates Pact\
→ community joins\
→ users complete program\
→ completion becomes social proof\
→ expert launches next cohort

## 13.4 Organization Loop

Employer sponsors Pact\
→ employees participate\
→ organization measures aggregate engagement\
→ another program is funded

# 14. Social Layer

Pact can create a lightweight social graph inside Vyana without turning the product into another feed.

Possible activity:

> **Shachindra started a Pact**

**21 days of meditation**\
17/21 days required\
7 people backing

Actions:

- **Back**
- **Join**
- **Send encouragement**

Progress should focus on commitment rather than health metrics.

For example:

> Day 11/21 ✓

rather than exposing the underlying biometric data.

A user's Pact profile can eventually represent:

- Commitments completed
- Current Pacts
- People backing them
- Pacts they are backing
- Programs completed
- Consistency streaks

This creates identity around **keeping commitments**, rather than around raw fitness performance.

# 15. Strategic Role Inside Seek Nirvana

Pact can become one of four reinforcing layers.

## 1. Understand

**Vyana**

Understand what your body and behavior are telling you.

## 2. Practice

**Sadhana / Programs**

Take small actions appropriate to your current state.

## 3. Commit

**Pact**

Turn the behavior you want into a commitment with accountability.

## 4. Support

**Experts & Community**

Get human guidance and social support when needed.

The broader journey becomes:

**Signal → Meaning → Practice → Commitment → Support → Change**

Pact should therefore not be treated primarily as a blockchain feature.

It can become the **commitment and accountability layer of the Seek Nirvana ecosystem.**

# 16. Business Questions to Validate

Before expanding aggressively into complex financial settlement, the product should validate the core behavior.

## 16.1 Retention

Do people create another Pact after completing their first one?

This indicates whether Pact can become a recurring behavioral loop.

## 16.2 Completion

Does putting something behind a commitment materially increase completion compared with a normal Vyana goal?

Compare:

- No stake
- Social stake
- Financial stake
- Sponsored reward

## 16.3 Social Acquisition

How many Pact creators invite another person?

How many invited users become active Vyana users?

The strongest viral metric may be:

> **New users acquired per Pact created**

## 16.4 Backing

Does allowing friends to back someone improve completion?

Does backing create more invitations than direct challenges?

This determines whether **Back Someone** should eventually become as important as **Challenge Someone**.

## 16.5 Organization Economics

Will organizations pay for:

- Program seats
- Reward pools
- Expert-led programs
- Completion verification
- Reward administration

If so, organization-sponsored Pact programs could become a major B2B product.

---

# 17. Initial Commercial Priorities

Pact can ultimately support many models, but the initial commercial priorities should remain narrow.

### Priority 1 — Retention

Use Pact to make Vyana practices more likely to become repeat habits.

### Priority 2 — Social Growth

Use challenges and backing to bring friends into the ecosystem naturally.

### Priority 3 — Program Completion

Attach Pacts to Seek Nirvana expert programs and cohorts.

### Priority 4 — B2B Sponsored Pacts

Allow organizations to fund programs and completion rewards without accessing personal health data.

### Priority 5 — Financial Settlement

Add financial Pacts where they improve accountability rather than making them the defining feature of the product.
